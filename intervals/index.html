Intervals and Harmonic Gravity

This section is emerging. It will explore why music feels settled, tense, clear, distorted, colorful, and alive.

Why start with harmonics?

Before notation, scales, or theory, there is vibration. A tone naturally contains relationships within itself: octave, fifth, third, and other overtones. Orumi begins here because harmonics may be the foundation of musical gravity.

The questions this section will explore

  • What makes one interval feel clear while another feels tense or distorted?
  • Why do some tones feel settled while others seem to pull toward resolution?
  • What is consonance? What is dissonance? What do they look and sound like?
  • Why has the tritone been called the "devil in music"?
  • What makes octaves, fifths, and tonic chords feel especially stable?
  • How do four notes interact inside a chord to create gravity, color, and motion?
  • How can sight reading become the recognition of movement through emotional and structural space?
  • How do chord transitions and phrasing create musical storytelling?

The working idea

Music is a play of tonality, emotion, color, and substance. Intervals are not just distances. They are relationships moving through harmonic space.
