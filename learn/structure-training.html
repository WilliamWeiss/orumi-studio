The Shape of a Song

Before a chord is right or wrong, it's early or late.

Every barbershop chart you've ever sung has a shape, whether or not anyone ever pointed it out. A song opens somewhere, settles into its main idea, and — if the arranger is any good — leaves you with one last moment stretched out a little longer than you expect.

That shape isn't decoration. A chord that sounds thrilling in the last four bars would sound show-offy in bar two, and a chord that sounds warm at the start would sound timid right before the finish. Knowing where you are in a piece is half of knowing what belongs there.

The hook. This is the melody people can hum without ever having sung the song — it tends to come back more than once, sometimes with small changes each time.

Tag tap to reveal

A few bars at the very end that repeat something already sung — often the last line, sometimes a phrase borrowed from earlier — before the final chord is allowed to fully land. Barbershoppers sing these on their own, apart from the song they came from.
