Orumi Studio

Explore harmony visually and relationally.

What is Orumi Studio?

Orumi Studio is an experimental musical sandbox where harmony becomes visible, interactive, and exploratory.

It is not trying to replace publication-quality notation software. It explores a different question: what if musical understanding could emerge through interaction, curiosity, experimentation, and play?

What Orumi Studio Builds

Orumi Studio develops tools, games, lessons, design experiments, and eventually physical blocks that help people experience music through relationships rather than symbols alone.

Harmony

Build chords and hear how tones settle, pull, brighten, darken, or create friction.

Intervals

Explore distance, direction, tension, resolution, and emotional gravity between tones.

Voice-leading

See how individual voices move through harmonic space, especially in four-part harmony.

Play

Use curiosity and experimentation as the central interaction model.

The Orumi Principle

Orumi should be simple at the surface and deep underneath. A child should be able to explore it, and a musician should be able to discover sophisticated structure within it.

Current Direction

The current Studio direction centers on four-part harmony and barbershop arranging. This gives Orumi a focused musical landscape where intervals, chord relationships, voice-leading, and harmonic gravity can be explored clearly.
