The Orumi Language

Orumi is being developed in public. These are active design principles, not a closed system.

What the Language Does

The Orumi language translates musical relationships into visible, interactive forms. It gives music shape, color, depth, duration, structure, and motion.

The Language page explains how Orumi represents music. The Intervals page explores why those relationships feel stable, tense, clear, distorted, settled, or in motion.

Core Design Ideas

Relationship

Notes gain meaning through context: home, distance, pull, friction, and release.

Visibility

Musical structure becomes visible as patterns, blocks, paths, and shapes.

Play

Exploration is not decoration. It is the central way Orumi invites learning.

Accessibility

Musical depth should feel approachable, intuitive, and human-centered.

Early Rules

  • Color represents note identity, voice, or sound character.
  • White represents rest or silence.
  • Length can represent duration.
  • Height can represent volume or emphasis.
  • Depth can represent octave.
  • A black top can represent sustain.
  • Shape and movement can represent musical direction, articulation, or expressive relationship.

The Orumi Principle

Orumi should be simple at the surface and deep underneath. The first interaction should invite curiosity. The hundredth interaction should still reveal something new.
